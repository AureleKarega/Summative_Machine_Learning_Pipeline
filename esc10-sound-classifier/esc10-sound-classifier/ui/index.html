Balanced classes. Every ESC-10 class has 40 clips, so accuracy is not inflated by a dominant class. Uploaded clips appear here and change the balance.

Brightness (spectral centroid). Rain (~3.5 kHz) is the brightest sound; dog, rooster, sneezing and helicopter sit around 1.3-1.6 kHz. Noise-like sounds spread energy into high frequencies, while barks, crows and rotor thumps are low and tonal.

Burstiness (RMS std). Clock tick, crackling fire and rain are steady (low variation), whereas dog and rooster are bursty (about 5-8x higher) because the sound comes and goes within the 5 s clip. This separates continuous from event-like sounds.

Energy (RMS mean). Helicopter and chainsaw are the loudest; clock tick and sneezing are quietest. Together with brightness this pulls apart sounds that share a pitch range, such as helicopter vs. dog.

Brightness vs. burstiness. Each dot is one clip. Classes form fairly distinct clusters, which is why a simple model on summary features reaches ~80% accuracy. Overlap (e.g. crackling fire and clock tick) explains most remaining errors.
